Loss review

Config sets the post-mortem threshold at $250K. April is the tell: 15 losses for $20K, an average of $1K each. Worth asking whether deals that small should be entering the pipeline at all.

The ledger exists to replace one pattern with another. Old: sales says a number, finance discounts it privately, sales feels audited, finance loses trust. New: both sides publish a number, the gap is discussed on Monday, and the evidence is HubSpot.

The operating rhythm

Daily, early AMFinance
HubSpot delta pull, company-touch and estimate-split queries, Daily Log regenerated newest-first, closed deals archived, new deals appended, amount-vs-estimate audit in the morning briefing. About five minutes, before the team is in the sheet. No re-sort, baselines untouched mid-week.
Fridays EODSellers
Sellers open their own tab only and update the judgment columns, biggest deals first: confidence, MAP, champion, signer, blocker / next step, notes. Every deal that moved gets a dated next step. Sizing lives in HubSpot Amount; estimates flow in automatically once delivered.
MondaysFinance + 1:1s
Full HubSpot truth-up (a complete pull catches the deletes and merges deltas cannot see), every tab re-sorted largest to smallest, weekly baseline re-locked, won-company set re-run for New / Existing Logo. Then the Weekly Log review, finance confidence, and the 1:1s.
First refresh of the monthClose
Monthly Log row freezes, Monthly Detail archives, the month baseline re-locks, and Closed Won / Lost is booked from HubSpot. Any loss at or above $250K gets a post-mortem row.

Who owns what

Sales owns

Sales confidence (H/M/L), MAP, champion, signer, blocker / next step, notes. That is the entire list — everything factual is automated.

Finance owns

Finance confidence (H/M/L), and the weights in Config. Nothing else.

HubSpot owns

Deal name, retailer, stage, sized $, new / existing logo, revenue type, estimate-delivered date, lead created, days since touch, days in stage. If HubSpot is wrong, fix HubSpot — the workbook follows.

What H, M and L mean

High  85%90-day close visibility
Named signer, a specific week, a written next step from the customer, and a forcing function.
Medium  55%Plausible, not committed
Most of the above, but one element is missing or was only verbal.
Low  25%Speculative
Commitment signals are present but the deal is not qualified against who, when, and what kills it.
Alignment gap±$100K triggers a talk
Finance-weighted minus sales-weighted. Negative means finance is the more conservative side. Any deal beyond ±$100K gets discussed on Monday — openly, in the room, not privately afterwards.

The five 1:1 questions Evidence first

1. What moved?
Weekly Log filtered to the seller: every stage change, estimate change and view change since Monday. Ask for the story behind each.
2. What's silent?
Any deal rated High or Medium with no touch in 14+ days. "It's going great" plus no logged activity anywhere is not going great.
3. Where do we disagree?
Every deal beyond ±$100K of alignment gap. Seller states their case, finance states theirs, and both agree what evidence would close the gap.
4. What slipped?
Target close and recognition dates versus last week. Slips are fine. Silent slips are not.
5. What's stuck?
Anything 60+ days in Auditing or later. What is the blocker, and is it written down in the blocker column?

Reading this page fairly

Three honest caveats. One: weighted dollars mix HubSpot facts with human judgment, and 54% of workable deals carry no sales confidence at all — the weighted numbers are floors, not forecasts. Two: days-since-touch is company-aware but partner email threads aren't linked to deal records, so partner-worked deals can look staler than they are; read stage age alongside it. Three: the recurring / DMS pipeline is outside this ledger and demonstrably holds closing business, so treat every total here as the direct-sales pipeline rather than the whole company.
